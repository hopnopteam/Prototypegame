<why_this_game_exists>
Night Express is built on a deep analysis of **My Perfect Hotel (MPH)** by Redux Games / SayGames — the most successful arcade idle game ever (300M+ downloads, $100M+ lifetime revenue). Everything below is derived from what made MPH win and where it is weak. Use this reasoning when making any decision the spec does not cover.

**Why MPH won (keep all of this):**
1. **A loop that pays out every few seconds.** Serve by hand → cash appears → spend it on an unlock tile → something new pops into existence. The next unlock is always 20–60 seconds away. There is never a dead moment.
2. **Hands-on, then hands-off.** You do every chore yourself first, then hire staff to automate it. Automation feels like the reward for effort, and it lets players stop without losing progress.
3. **Money you can see and touch.** Cash physically stacks on the floor; you walk over it to scoop it; you stand on a price tile and watch the cash drain into it.
4. **Zero learning cost.** No tutorial text. One finger, a floating joystick, walk-over-to-act. Understood in 5 seconds.
5. **A hybrid economy tuned for the pain points.** Rewarded ads appear exactly when the player feels a shortage (short on cash, too slow, missing a resource). Hard currency (gems) can replace *any* rewarded ad, so ads and purchases never fight. A no-ads pack is the best seller.
6. **Content that scales cheaply.** New locations reuse the same systems with new art and a few new jobs, which makes live ops, seasonal events and brand collaborations (MPH's Barbie event) affordable.

**Where MPH is weak (we must beat it here):**
1. **Forced ads mid-task** every 1–3 minutes — the #1 complaint in reviews; players uninstall over it.
2. **Repetition after the third hotel** — new locations add new skins, not new verbs.
3. **No ending beat** — the loop never resolves; nothing inside a session feels "done".
4. **Late-game slowdown** — progress currency becomes a grind.
5. **Per-hotel passes** — purchases stop applying when you move, which feels like a trick.
6. **Faceless people** — guests and staff have no personality until a licensed IP was imported.
7. **Performance** — stutters on busy scenes on mid-range phones.
8. **Walking time as a tax** — far-apart amenities exist to sell speed boosts.

**Market reality (2026):** idle is mature and crowded; publishers now sign only a familiar loop *plus one genuinely new structural idea*, with strong long-term retention. Pure theme swaps (cruise ship, pet hotel, small "train hotel" apps) exist and have all failed to scale. Our new idea is the **journey rhythm** and the **train that physically grows** — protect those above everything else.

**Targets the prototype must be built to hit** (publisher go/no-go numbers): Day-1 retention 40%+, Day-7 10%+, Day-0 playtime 25+ minutes.
</why_this_game_exists>

<game_design>

## 1. One-line pitch
Run a luxury sleeper train that grows carriage by carriage as it crosses the world.

## 2. Player fantasy
"I started with one rusty carriage and turned it into the most famous luxury train in the world." Cozy, bustling, a little romantic — travel, scenery, night lights, the whistle, the clack of the rails.

## 3. Camera, orientation, controls
- **Portrait**, one-handed.
- **The train runs diagonally across the screen (session 12, owner request):** locomotive toward the top right, rear toward the bottom left, at a configurable angle (`trainYawDeg`, 30°; it reads about 37° on screen). The world is not rotated: the camera orbits the train's frame, so every system keeps its train-axis coordinates. A three-quarter dollhouse view looks down 48° through a narrow 33° lens, roofs cut away so the rooms read like a model on a table; it follows the player on both axes with smooth damping (session 13: it used to hold the train's centre line, so walking across the carriage looked like sliding sideways), leads a little in the direction of travel the same way in every direction, leans slightly out over the lake, and never shows past the train's ends (`endMargin`). All of it is in `src/config/visuals.ts`.
- **The train is stationary in world space; the scenery moves.** Lakeside scenery scrolls past both sides of the train (session 12, made continuous in session 13): the lake on the upper-left side, forest, farms and villages on the other. This keeps physics and AI simple and stable.
- **Drag anywhere to walk** (session 26, owner: "remove the UI movement thing… just player move but without showing the joystick": a floating joystick still reads the drag, but nothing is drawn), read relative to the screen (push up, walk up the screen) and turned into a floor direction through the tilted view, so pushing up walks straight up the screen (session 13, owner: "I move ahead and it goes either left or right"); only directions within 12° of the train's length or width snap onto it, easing back to the exact direction by 24°, so corridors stay easy without bending a diagonal push (`axisSnapDeg`). The conductor faces the way they actually move, and keeps pace when sliding along a wall (`player.wallGlide`). No buttons for core actions — everything is walk-over-to-act (come to a job and the ring round its badge fills, the action happens; session 27: nothing is drawn on the floor, and near the job's spot, near the thing its badge floats over, or anywhere in its room all count, never through a wall; §6 "Job signs").
- Player carries items as a visible **stack** (towels, tea trays, luggage, coal) up to a capacity limit.

## 4. The core loop (moment to moment)
Guests buy a ticket at the booth (cash appears) → walk to their room → one request in the evening → lights out → one request in the morning → alight at their stop, leaving a tip and a used room → the room is tidied in one visit before the next guest → cash is spent on unlock tiles (new cabins, amenities, carriages, staff, upgrades) → more guests, more income. (Session 22: one trip, one sleep, §4b; session 23: one-visit tidy again.)

**Verbs in the first route:**
- **Sell tickets at the ticket stand (session 22; rebuilt in session 23, owner: "when you collect tickets NPCs push the player")**: a ticket stand on every platform just ahead of the lobby door. You serve from its outer side (nearest the camera, never hidden); travellers queue on its train side in a line along the platform edge, pay at the window and walk back along the edge to the door, so nobody's path crosses yours, and the conductor is never pushed by anyone (`Crowd`: `PLAYER_GIVE` 0). Paid travellers walk aboard to the reception desk (session 24, below; nobody who paid is left behind). Latecomers come out of the station house and walk in along the walkway. Layout: `world/platformLayout.ts`.
- **"All aboard!" (session 33, owner: "the bell-ringing mechanic feels almost pointless; give it a meaningful purpose or remove it"; replaces the latecomers below):** the bell is ready only once the platform is done (everyone with a bed aboard, no bags left on the barrow, or the rack full) and more than `bell.before` (8) seconds are left; ringing it closes the doors `bell.leaveIn` (3) seconds later and pays `bell.bonusPerSecond` (0.5) × carriages for every second saved, as an on-time bonus over the bell (a clock icon and the amount). A clean, quick stop is worth more and the next leg comes sooner; staying lets the player buy at the station workshop or collect cash first. The guide points at it after any affordable station upgrade. Code: `Station.platformDone`/`bellReady`/`ringBell`, `Journey.departIn`.
- **Ring the station bell (session 29, superseded in session 33, partner: "more things to do in the early game… making announcements at stations"):** from the second stop a brass hand bell hangs from an iron post on the platform, just behind the door, out of everyone's way. Once a stop, while more than 16 s are left on the clock, the conductor stands by it a second: "ding-ding", everyone waiting looks up and two latecomers hurry out of the station house, one after the other, to buy tickets (more guests, more fares). The guide points at it first when the doors open; a lesson ("Ring the bell") teaches it once; it counts for Rush. Staff never ring it, so a hired porter leaves the conductor this beat of the stop. Tunables: `bell` (seconds, before, travellers, stagger) and `flow.features.bell` in `economy.ts`; spot `PLATFORM.bell`/`bellPad`; code: `Station.bellReady`/`ringBell`, `buildBellPost` in `PlatformView`, `Guests.lookUp`, the `bell` icon, event `station.bell`.
- **Hand keys at the desk (session 24, owner: "the booth sells the fare; the on-board desk checks guests in with a cabin key… sells extras… no duplicated income")**: every ticketed guest queues at the lobby's desk; stand on its blue pad (0.9 s) and they are handed the key to a made-up room of their class (a key pops over them, a ding) and walk to it; nobody goes to a room without one, and nobody pays twice (the fare was the stand's). The first in line shows a key bubble once a room of their class is ready, a "no room" sign until then. **The desk's extra:** once a venue is open, about a third of guests book a table for the evening as they check in (the venue's icon over them, a small booking fee at the desk: 5 × their class's tip multiplier), and their evening outing goes to that venue when a seat is free; they still pay at the venue. The Porter sells tickets at the stand during stops and hands keys whenever nobody waits there. In the opening, "Hand keys" is the walkthrough's last step, after "Open". Lost property and complaints were left out (they would add a carry step to the one-visit tidy, and complaints already show as the soft cues). Tunables: `zones.checkInSeconds`, `desk` in `economy.ts`; code: `Guests.boardPaid`/`deskStay`/`checkIn`/`hasGuestAtDesk`, `Guest.booked`, `Venues.bookable`, `Staff.porterTask`, coach step `key`, the `key` icon, event `guest.keyed` (Rush and the check-in goals count it).
- **Check in at the middle of the desk (session 38, owner: "the check-in prompt is still placed weirdly… in the middle of the desk, with a bigger area where it registers"; "customers don't check in… after I upgraded to Comfort"):** the key badge hangs over the middle of the desk top (`ZoneOptions.badgeAt`, `deskBadge` in `TrainState`) and anywhere within 0.75 m of the desk counts (`area`, `DESK_AREA_MARGIN`); the coach's "Check in" line hangs above the badge, never over it (`CoachAnchor.label`/`height`). **The stuck guests:** a guest waits at the desk for a free room of their class, and a class refit can leave the train with no room of a ticketed guest's class at all (the only Basic carriage became Comfort), so they waited for ever. Now a guest whose class has no open room left is handed a free room of the nearest class above, on the house (`TrainState.roomFor`, used by `deskReady`/`deskStay`/`callForward` and the key bubble).
- **Food on request (session 38, owner: "players should also request food, picked up at the same place as coffee… real food… the tray reflects the item… food visible on the coffee table"):** a new item, `sandwich` (a club sandwich in two triangles on a white plate, the lettuce and ham showing, a stick and an olive), asked for in the evening by Basic and Comfort guests and as an extra by Basic, Comfort and Business; picked up at the tea counter (`SERVICE_ITEMS`, `sourceAnchorFor`), where a plate of them stands on a little brass cake stand beside the urn and the papers, with china cups on saucers and a sugar bowl (`sandwichPlate` in `CarriageView`); its icon, praise ("Just what I needed!") and "Bring sandwich" line. Venue staff and the attendant fetch it like tea.
- **Tidy rooms in one visit (session 23, owner: "make it like before, just don't let guests sleep multiple times in one night")**: session 22's bedding swap (strip, linen cupboard, make the bed) is gone. A used room is tidied in one visit to its pad (~2.6 s) and is ready for the next guest; the one-night rule (§4b) stays. The Attendant does it. Code: `TrainState.cleanStay`, `finishTurnaround`.
- **Clean cabins** after guests alight: one broom pad in the heart of the room, ~2.6 s (session 9: the small mat is gone; the room's own floor carries the tier, see §6). **Every guest leaves their own mess** (session 8, owner request; unified in session 17, owner: "the dirt, unclean room presentation is still a mess… doesn't look thrown together"): a used room is one composition in every room, in that room's own colours: the bed they slept in (the duvet kicked down into a roll at the foot with one corner thrown back, the creased white sheet showing) and one thing of theirs on the floor beside the bed, always in the same place (a businessman's paper, a backpacker's map, grandma's yarn, newlyweds' petals, a family's teddy, a VIP's feather boa; now and then something anyone might leave). Pieces are drawn at 1.35× (they were 1.9×, toy-box sized, three to a room in random corners). The broom icon floats over every room waiting to be tidied (session 30, owner: "the tidy sign sometimes disappears and is confusing"; it used to show only over the room the guide pointed at or the conductor was beside). The conductor sweeps with a broom; the piece hops in to the broom and pops with a puff, the bed smooths flat last: then a sparkle and "Spotless!". **Session 23 (owner: "the dirty animation… make it more detailed, actually dynamic"):** a room turns untidy over a moment as its guest leaves (the duvet kicked down, the pillow knocked askew, their thing dropped with a bounce, litter popping onto the floor round the pad: a crumpled paper ball, a paper cup, a banana peel, a sweet wrapper, a dust bunny, 3–4 of them, drawn 1.35×); tidying plays in order with the pad's fill: the litter swept in to the broom one bit after another, their thing picked up, the pillow put straight with a plump, the thrown-back corner turned down, the sheet smoothed and the duvet pulled up the bed (`CarriageView.playMessArrival`/`poseMess`, `Mess.ts` `bedMessParts`/`buildMessPillow`/`buildLitter`; preview `?clean=0.5` or `?clean=arrive`). Tunables: `mess` in `src/config/economy.ts`, pools in `ARCHETYPES`. A small tip left behind.
- **Carry and restock**: towels and toilet rolls from the supply car to bathrooms; tea to the dining car.
- **Fulfil requests**: a guest shows a bubble; bring it for bonus stars and a tip. Session 22: one in the evening and one in the morning, never the same thing twice (§4b).
- **Load luggage** at station stops.
- **Collect cash**: cash stacks where it was earned; walk over to scoop it (the most satisfying moment — make it feel amazing).
- **Serve in the venues (session 20)**: resting guests take outings to the café, the dining car, the bar and the dome; brew, cook, mix or show them in at the venue's station, carry it over and hand it to them; clear the dining tables after each diner (§6c).
- **Unlock**: stand on a price tile; cash flies from the player into the tile until it completes.

## 4b. One trip, one sleep (session 22, co-founder request; protect this)
A guest's ride is one night, and its parts follow how far through the ride they are (0 boarding, 1 pulling into their stop), never going back (`sim/trip.ts`, unit-tested):

| Part | What happens |
|---|---|
| Boarding | ticket at the booth, bags to the porter, walk to the room |
| Settling in (evening) | the bed is already made; one request from the class's evening list, then perhaps an outing (a venue that suits the evening, or the washroom, at most once a trip) |
| Lights out (night) | once per guest: they lie down, the room dims (`world/RoomDimmer.ts`), the blind comes down, Zzz; no requests |
| Wake-up (morning) | lights up, blind up, a stretch; one request from the morning list, perhaps an outing |
| Arrival | they get off at their stop, leaving a tip and a used room |

- **Late deliveries cost (session 33, owner: "consequences for failing to deliver items to passengers on time… anger, smaller tips, or no tips at all… communicated through NPC dialogue or other clear feedback… faster deliveries should obviously result in better rewards"):** a request's tip follows how long it took (`service` in `economy.ts`): within 8 s ×1.6 with a bolt and "Speedy!", within 16 s ×1.25 and "Thanks!", up to 30 s the base tip; from 30 s half, a frown and "Finally…"; from 48 s nothing, an angry face, a stomp, a grumble and "Too slow!". While they wait, the bubble turns red and they check their watch from 18 s, and at 30 s an angry face pops over them once with a grumble, so the player sees time running out. Late and very late deliveries dip the service mood (the Train Rating's service share). Never during the first-passengers tutorial. Code: `Guests.patience`/`fulfil`, `Guest.crossAt`, `Feedback.onSlowDelivery`.
- **Passengers cheer good service (session 35, owner: "good reactions from the passengers… emoji or words that give that dopamine rush when they do things right"):** whatever the conductor does for someone well gets a bouncy gold word with a heart over them (`praise` floats, `.float.praise` with a pop-in): a request within 8 s "Speedy!"/"Lightning!" with the bolt, within 16 s a word for the thing brought ("Perfect cuppa!", "So cosy!", "So soft!", "So fluffy!", "Chocolate!") or "Lovely!"/"Perfect!", in good time "Thank you!"/"Cheers!"; a key handed over, a room tidied ("Spotless!"), shoes shined ("Shiny!") and each venue's order ("Yum!", "Divine!", "Bravo!") too. Each pool is used in turn, so no game randomness is spent; staff work stays quiet. Data: `PRAISE` in `config/chatter.ts`; code: `Feedback.praise` (listens to `guest.keyed`, `cabin.cleaned`, `shoes.shined`, `venue.served`), `Guests.fulfil`.
- **Guests keep asking (session 30, owner: "the passengers go quite without asking for anything for a long time, we should have interactivity most of the game"):** once a guest's evening or morning request has been seen to, 85% of them ask for one more thing 5–9 s later from their class's `extra` list (Basic: a pillow, tea, the paper or a blanket; never one they already had). Tunables: `trip.again`, `extra` in `config/classes.ts`; code: `Guest.again`/`againNow`, `Guests.extraRequests`.

| Class | Evening | Morning |
|---|---|---|
| Basic | an extra blanket | a cup of tea, or the morning paper (session 28: a stack of papers lies on the counter beside the urn, where it is picked up, so the request makes sense; session 26: the wake-up call, an alarm clock meaning "stand at their door", read as a timer with nothing to do) |
| Comfort | tea | a towel |
| Business | the paper | coffee |
| First | champagne, and the turndown with a chocolate on arrival | breakfast |
| Royal | champagne, and the turndown | the butler: breakfast and coffee, one generous tip |

- **Everyone gets off at their stop (session 27, owner: "passengers who requested coffee but didn't receive it never went to their rooms or completed their journey… rooms remaining occupied… passengers should always be able to offboard… final payment reduced… an unhappy sign when they leave"):** the cause was a guest waiting in a venue's queue or at a table for an order that never came: no state there checked for their stop, so they stayed aboard for good and kept their room. Now, when their stop comes and the doors open, a guest drops whatever they are doing (a venue's queue or table, a washroom or the way to one, walking back to their room) and gets off (`Guests.leaveForStop`, `Venues.abandon`: what they ate is paid, their seat or place in the queue freed). Every service they asked for and never got (an open request, a venue order they had waited at least `guests.unmetAfterSeconds` (15) for, a venue or a popcorn they gave up on) takes `guests.unmetTipCut` (half) off the tip they leave, down to nothing; they leave with a frown over them all the way to the station house, a grumble, and (once the soft cues are on) a dip in the service mood, which is the Train Rating's service share (`feedback.mood.unhappy`). A venue keeps nobody waiting for ever: after `VENUE_TUNING.patience` (60 s) in its queue, at a table for their order or in the cinema for a film, a guest gives up with a frown and goes back to their room (a popcorn or blanket that never came is given up on and they stay). Audit: `Guests.audit.overstayed` (anyone still aboard past their stop as the doors close; the smoke run requires 0) and `unhappy`. Measured with the café never served for 14 minutes on the autopilot: nobody overstayed, the queue empty at every stop.
- The evening ends at 40% of the ride, morning starts at 64% (each guest moved by up to ±8%, so a carriage never goes quiet at once), the shortest night 4 s; a request left open at bedtime is finished first. Tunables: `trip` in `economy.ts`, `evening`/`morning` in `config/classes.ts`, venues' `when` in `config/venues.ts`.
- Every phase change writes a debug line (`[Trip] guest 12 (backpacker, basic): settling in → lights out at 41%`, dev tools on); the smoke run audits 20 minutes: no guest sleeps twice, no repeated or out-of-place request, every used room tidied.
- **The night shift (session 24, owner: "the player should almost never be idle… night work pays off in the morning… don't make the game bloated"):** at lights out a guest leaves their shoes outside the door (a scuffed brown pair, drawn 1.4×, a blue pad with a shoe on it in the corridor beside them); stand there 1.3 s to polish them (a brisk rub, sparkles, they gleam). In the morning, or as they get off if they slept through, polished shoes leave a tip in the room (4 × their class's tip multiplier); unpolished ones are simply taken in. **Night owls:** a quarter of guests stay up 14% of the ride longer and ask for one more thing first, a nightcap (tea or a blanket they have not had). Bedtimes spread wider (`trip.jitter` 0.12, was 0.08), so shoes and wake-ups come one after another. The Attendant now does what its tile says (cleans, restocks); answering requests and polishing shoes come with its training ("Faster Attendant": "Serves guests too", `staff.attendantServesFromLevel`), so hiring help never leaves the conductor idle. The cruise is 120 s (was 150): stations, the busiest part of play, come more often. A lesson "Shine shoes" teaches it the first time. Measured on the autopilot (two seeds, 20 minutes, after the first minute): idle 52–53% → 17–26% (nights 75–85% → 11–23%); 24–26 purchases → 31–34. Tunables: `night`, `trip.owl`, `trip.jitter`, `journey.moveSeconds`, `staff.attendantServesFromLevel`; code: `Cabin.shoes`/`shoePad`/`shoeSpot`, `TrainState.leaveShoes`/`takeShoes`/`shoesToShine`/`shineStay`, `world/Shoes.ts`, `Guest.owlWish`/`owlNow`, the `shoe` icon, guidance reason `shine`, coach lesson `shoes`, event `shoes.shined` (Rush counts it).

## 5. THE TWIST — Journey rhythm (protect this)
The game alternates between two phases on a loop:

| Phase | Default length | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **On the move** | ~120 s (tunable; 150 until session 24) | Scenery scrolls, rails clack. Serve cabins, requests, dining car, bathrooms. Classic MPH loop. |
| **Arriving** | ~6 s | Train slows, a platform slides in on the right side, whistle, station name banner. |
| **Station stop** | ~40 s (tunable) | A rush: board the platform queue, load luggage onto the luggage car, restock supplies from the platform vendor, guests whose stop it is alight (tips!). A departure clock is visible. |
| **Departing** | ~6 s | Doors close, whistle, platform slides away, speed builds. **This is the only place a forced (interstitial) ad may ever appear.** |

Rules:
- **Bags left behind cost something (session 30, owner: "leaving the baggage of the customer should have some sort of penalty or reduced fares… communicated so it actually feels important"):** each bag still on the barrow as the doors close is sent on after its owner at the train's cost: `luggage.refundPerBag` × the owner's class tip multiplier is taken from the wallet (never below zero), the owner counts a service missed (a smaller tip and a frown when they get off), a crossed bag and the refund float over the barrow with a grumble, and the station card adds "2 left −20" in red (`Station.chargeLeftBags`, `StationResult.bagsLeft/bagRefund`). Not when the rack is full.
- **Consequences, always recoverable (fix list 4.2; this replaces the old "no failure state, no consequences" rule at the owner's request):** nothing ends the game or takes away what you own, but how well a stop goes now counts. The **Train Rating** (five stars on the route line) is the average score of the last five stops (everyone with a bed boarded 60%, the bags loaded 20%, guests served without a long wait 20%; a new game starts at 3★). High, it brings fuller platforms (up to 2 more travellers), richer classes (aspirants ×2) and tips ×1.25; low, emptier platforms (65% of the crowd), fewer aspirants (×0.5) and tips ×0.8. A few good stops always bring it back. **Missed travellers (fix list 4.1):** a traveller who had a bed but was not boarded storms off angry (a frown, a stomp along the platform) and the fare is lost; the result card shows it and the rating change. Tunables: `rating` in `economy.ts`; code: `sim/stationResult.ts` (`stopQuality`, `trainRating`, `ratingEffect`, unit-tested), `Station` (`route.recentStops`, a save field filled by `mergeDefaults`), `Guests.stormOff`, `Ui.updateRating`.
- **The station clock (fix list 4.3):** big in the middle of the route line, yellow under 20 s and red under 10 s, a tick each of the last ten seconds and a short warning whistle at ten (`journey.timer`; `Station.updateClock`).
- A **station bonus** rewards a clean stop (everyone boarded, luggage loaded) — a small chest + star burst, never a penalty.
- Stations give the session a heartbeat: a peak every ~3 minutes and a breather in between. They also solve MPH's "no ending beat": each stop is a mini-finale with a result card (guests boarded, tips earned, stars).
- Rewarded ad unique to our design: **"Hold the train" (+15 s at a station)**. While monetization is off (fix list 1) it is earned: free at last call from route level 3 (`perks.holdTrainLevel`).
- **Boarding needs beds (session 5):** only travellers with a free bed board; the rest wait on the platform with a "no room" bubble. They never count against a perfect stop, and the result ticket says how many were left behind: visible demand for more cabins, never a penalty.
- **The result card (session 26: compact, the stars and the headline on one line and the numbers on the next, 4 s; owner: "the UI feels way too cluttered") (session 24, owner: "one big card… headline, 3 stars, Boarded 10/10, total earned, bonus, no other text"):** as the doors close, one card in the middle column: a headline band whose words follow the stars (session 28, owner: "if we get 1 star or 2 it should say… the negative, middle or average through words"): three stars read "All aboard!" on gold for a perfect stop ("Great stop!" otherwise), two "Not bad" in slate, one "Rough stop" in red on a pale red card (`RESULT_HEADLINES` in `Ui.ts`, `.average`/`.poor`), three stars that pop in one after another (one for the stop, one when everyone with a bed boarded, one when the bags were loaded; what was missed stays grey), "Boarded 3/3" (with the bags and the no-room count as icon chips), what the stop earned (fares sold plus tips left) and the bonus. **Perfect stops** play a jingle with the chest (`perfect`), throw confetti at the door and over the conductor, and build a **perfect streak**: each one in a row adds 25% to the bonus, up to ×2.5 (a "×3" chip on the card); any other stop just starts the count again. **Missed travellers** (once the soft cues are on): the camera glances back at them on the platform for 2.2 s, waving their tickets ("wait for me!"), a fond "aww" plays, and the card adds one soft red line, "2 missed" and the fares they would have paid ("−40"): shown, never taken. A celebration already moving the camera (a carriage rolling in) keeps it. Tunables: `money.perfectStreakStep`/`perfectStreakMax`, `feedback.missedGlide`; code: `sim/stationResult.ts` (`stopRating`, `nextStreak`, `streakBonus`, unit-tested), `Station.finishStop`, `Guests.estimatedFare`, `Ui.showResult` (`.ticket.result`), the `waveTicket` action with its `ticket` hand prop, cues `perfect` and `aww`; save `route.perfectStreak` (filled by `mergeDefaults`).

## 9. Progression — three nested clocks
1. **Seconds:** the next unlock tile (always 20–60 s away).
2. **Session:** the **route level** star bar. Every unlock, upgrade, request and clean station stop adds stars; a full bar levels the route and pays Rail Miles + cash (x2 with a rewarded ad). Route 1 maxes at level 8.
3. **Days:** new routes (roughly one every 1–2 hours of play), passenger stories, the postcard album, the season pass.

Introduce each layer only after the one below it is understood:
- **Session 5 (owner: "tight, addictive progression"):** level 2 comes at ~6:30; stars fly from where they were earned into the level ring, which fills as they land; near a level-up one toast says what the next level brings. **Rush:** services done by hand back to back (within 8 s) build a streak shown under the conductor, and milestones pay a cash bonus; a lapse costs nothing. ## Architecture principles
1. **Data-driven.** Every number that designers tune (prices, timers, speeds, capacities, rewards, ad rules) lives in ScriptableObjects or a single economy table — never hard-coded. Changing balance must not require code changes.
2. **Modular and decoupled.** Systems talk through a lightweight event bus or C# events and interfaces. No god objects, no scene-wide `FindObjectOfType` in gameplay loops.
3. **Service interfaces for everything external.** Ads, IAP, analytics and remote config sit behind interfaces (`IAdService`, `IIAPService`, `IAnalyticsService`, `IRemoteConfig`) with **mock implementations** now; the publisher's SDKs plug in later without touching gameplay code.
4. **Performance by default.** Object pooling for guests, cash, particles and items; no per-frame allocations in hot paths; cached component references; GPU instancing for repeated props; baked or minimal lighting; simple colliders.
5. **Deterministic and testable** where cheap: pure C# classes for economy math and state machines, so they can be unit tested with the Unity Test Framework.
6. **Save system** from day one: JSON with a **version number and migrations**, autosave on key events and on app pause/quit, and **offline earnings** on return (capped, with a x2 rewarded option).
