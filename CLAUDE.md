<why_this_game_exists>
Night Express is built on a deep analysis of **My Perfect Hotel (MPH)** by Redux Games / SayGames — the most successful arcade idle game ever (300M+ downloads, $100M+ lifetime revenue). Everything below is derived from what made MPH win and where it is weak. Use this reasoning when making any decision the spec does not cover.

**Why MPH won (keep all of this):**
1. **A loop that pays out every few seconds.** Serve by hand → cash appears → spend it on an unlock tile → something new pops into existence. The next unlock is always 20–60 seconds away. There is never a dead moment.
2. **Hands-on, then hands-off.** You do every chore yourself first, then hire staff to automate it. Automation feels like the reward for effort, and it lets players stop without losing progress.
3. **Money you can see and touch.** Cash physically stacks on the floor; you walk over it to scoop it; you stand on a price tile and watch the cash drain into it.
4. **Zero learning cost.** No tutorial text. One finger, a floating joystick, walk-over-to-act. Understood in 5 seconds.
5. **A hybrid economy tuned for the pain points.** Rewarded ads appear exactly when the player feels a shortage (short on cash, too slow, missing a resource). Hard currency (gems) can replace *any* rewarded ad, so ads and purchases never fight. A no-ads pack is the best seller.
6. **Content that scales cheaply.** New locations reuse the same systems with new art and a few new jobs, which makes live ops, seasonal events and brand collaborations (MPH's Barbie event) affordable.

**Where MPH is weak (we must beat it here):**
1. **Forced ads mid-task** every 1–3 minutes — the #1 complaint in reviews; players uninstall over it.
2. **Repetition after the third hotel** — new locations add new skins, not new verbs.
3. **No ending beat** — the loop never resolves; nothing inside a session feels "done".
4. **Late-game slowdown** — progress currency becomes a grind.
5. **Per-hotel passes** — purchases stop applying when you move, which feels like a trick.
6. **Faceless people** — guests and staff have no personality until a licensed IP was imported.
7. **Performance** — stutters on busy scenes on mid-range phones.
8. **Walking time as a tax** — far-apart amenities exist to sell speed boosts.

**Market reality (2026):** idle is mature and crowded; publishers now sign only a familiar loop *plus one genuinely new structural idea*, with strong long-term retention. Pure theme swaps (cruise ship, pet hotel, small "train hotel" apps) exist and have all failed to scale. Our new idea is the **journey rhythm** and the **train that physically grows** — protect those above everything else.

**Targets the prototype must be built to hit** (publisher go/no-go numbers): Day-1 retention 40%+, Day-7 10%+, Day-0 playtime 25+ minutes.
</why_this_game_exists>

<game_design>

## 1. One-line pitch
Run a luxury sleeper train that grows carriage by carriage as it crosses the world.

## 2. Player fantasy
"I started with one rusty carriage and turned it into the most famous luxury train in the world." Cozy, bustling, a little romantic — travel, scenery, night lights, the whistle, the clack of the rails.

## 3. Camera, orientation, controls
- **Portrait**, one-handed.
- **The train runs diagonally across the screen (session 12, owner request):** locomotive toward the top right, rear toward the bottom left, at a configurable angle (`trainYawDeg`, 30°; it reads about 37° on screen). The world is not rotated: the camera orbits the train's frame, so every system keeps its train-axis coordinates. A three-quarter dollhouse view looks down 48° through a narrow 33° lens, roofs cut away so the rooms read like a model on a table; it follows the player on both axes with smooth damping (session 13: it used to hold the train's centre line, so walking across the carriage looked like sliding sideways), leads a little in the direction of travel the same way in every direction, leans slightly out over the lake, and never shows past the train's ends (`endMargin`). All of it is in `src/config/visuals.ts`.
- **The train is stationary in world space; the scenery moves.** Lakeside scenery scrolls past both sides of the train (session 12, made continuous in session 13): the lake on the upper-left side, forest, farms and villages on the other. This keeps physics and AI simple and stable.
- **Floating joystick** anywhere on screen, read relative to the screen (push up, walk up the screen) and turned into a floor direction through the tilted view, so pushing up walks straight up the screen (session 13, owner: "I move ahead and it goes either left or right"); only directions within 12° of the train's length or width snap onto it, easing back to the exact direction by 24°, so corridors stay easy without bending a diagonal push (`axisSnapDeg`). The conductor faces the way they actually move, and keeps pace when sliding along a wall (`player.wallGlide`). No buttons for core actions — everything is walk-over-to-act (stand in a zone, a radial fill completes, the action happens).
- Player carries items as a visible **stack** (towels, tea trays, luggage, coal) up to a capacity limit.

## 4. The core loop (moment to moment)
Guests board → pay their fare (cash appears) → go to a cabin → make requests during the ride → sleep → alight at their stop, leaving a tip → the cabin needs cleaning before the next guest → cash is spent on unlock tiles (new cabins, amenities, carriages, staff, upgrades) → more guests, more income.

**Verbs in the first route:**
- **Sell fares / check in** at the boarding desk in the first passenger carriage (cash).
- **Clean cabins** after guests alight: one broom pad in the heart of the room, ~2.6 s (session 9: the small mat is gone; the room's own floor carries the tier, see §6). **Every guest leaves their own mess** (session 8, owner request): 2–3 pieces from their archetype's pool (a businessman's papers and cup, a backpacker's map and socks, grandma's yarn and book, newlyweds' petals and champagne, a family's toys, a VIP's feather boa), sometimes one anyone might leave, on the boards around the mat, plus one of three unmade beds (heap, tangle, kicked off). The conductor sweeps with a broom; each piece hops in to the broom and pops with a puff, the bed smooths flat last and the mat brightens: then a sparkle and "Spotless!". Tunables: `mess` in `src/config/economy.ts`, pools in `ARCHETYPES`. A small tip left behind.
- **Carry and restock**: towels and toilet rolls from the supply car to bathrooms; tea to the dining car.
- **Fulfil requests**: a guest shows a bubble (blanket, tea, pillow); bring it for bonus stars and a tip.
- **Load luggage** at station stops.
- **Collect cash**: cash stacks where it was earned; walk over to scoop it (the most satisfying moment — make it feel amazing).
- **Unlock**: stand on a price tile; cash flies from the player into the tile until it completes.

## 5. THE TWIST — Journey rhythm (protect this)
The game alternates between two phases on a loop:

| Phase | Default length | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **On the move** | ~150 s (tunable) | Scenery scrolls, rails clack. Serve cabins, requests, dining car, bathrooms. Classic MPH loop. |
| **Arriving** | ~6 s | Train slows, a platform slides in on the right side, whistle, station name banner. |
| **Station stop** | ~40 s (tunable) | A rush: board the platform queue, load luggage onto the luggage car, restock supplies from the platform vendor, guests whose stop it is alight (tips!). A departure clock is visible. |
| **Departing** | ~6 s | Doors close, whistle, platform slides away, speed builds. **This is the only place a forced (interstitial) ad may ever appear.** |

Rules:
- **No failure state, ever.** Guests who don't board in time simply wait for the next train; nothing is lost except a bonus.
- A **station bonus** rewards a clean stop (everyone boarded, luggage loaded) — a small chest + star burst, never a penalty.
- Stations give the session a heartbeat: a peak every ~3 minutes and a breather in between. They also solve MPH's "no ending beat": each stop is a mini-finale with a result card (guests boarded, tips earned, stars).
- Rewarded ad unique to our design: **"Hold the train" (+15 s at a station)**.
- **Boarding needs beds (session 5):** only travellers with a free bed board; the rest wait on the platform with a "no room" bubble. They never count against a perfect stop, and the result ticket says how many were left behind: visible demand for more cabins, never a penalty.
- **Soft cues, never penalties (session 6, owner request):** from route level 2 (or five stops) a passenger who had a bed but was never boarded, a request left waiting 30 s, a washroom out of towels or a guest stood at the desk with a room ready gets noticed: a grey note ("Missed", "Slow service", "No towels"), a soft "wah-wah", a grumble or the desk bell, and the ticket says "2 missed the train" in red. Nothing is taken away. Tunables: `feedback` in `src/config/economy.ts`.
- **Station workshop (session 5, owner request):** while the train is in, two pads on the platform sell **exterior** upgrades (window boxes, brass lamps, gold lining, nameboards with the train's name, a red carpet that rolls out of the lobby door) and **marketing** (posters of your train on every platform, roadside billboards in the countryside, a brass band at the door). Each one is visible on the train or the platform and adds a bonus (tips, fares, more travellers, more VIPs, a bigger station bonus). Data: `STATION_UPGRADES` in `src/config/content.ts`.

## 6. The train grows (protect this)
- New carriages are unlocked from a price tile at the **rear coupling** of the train. When it completes, the new carriage rolls in from off-screen and couples on with a satisfying "clunk", dust, camera shake and a fanfare. This is our signature progress moment and our main ad creative.
- **You choose what joins (session 5, owner request):** buying a coupling opens a chooser with up to three carriage cards (bathroom, supply, sleeper, luggage, within each type's limit), the best pick first with a reason: a washroom first, then its supplies, then beds if guests were left behind, racks if bags were. Route 1 holds five carriages, so every pick is a real choice. Each type brings its own tiles (`CARRIAGE_CATALOGUE`, generated into the chain by `buildUnlocks`).
- **Carriage types** (introduced gradually across routes): Sleeper (cabins), Bathroom car, Luggage car, Supply car, Dining car + kitchen, Bar lounge, Observation dome, Spa car, Cinema car, Kids' play car, First-class suites.
- Each carriage has internal unlock tiles (cabins, beds, sinks, tables) and upgrade levels (cosmetic + revenue).
- **Floors tell the story (session 9, owner request: "we NEED broken floors that look real… every upgrade adds a new layer"):** tier 0 is real 3D grey planks with open seams over a dark subfloor and joists; every room and corridor has its own damage (missing boards you can see into, splintered ends, a split board, a warped board lifting), rusty nails and cobwebs in the corners. Tier 1 repairs exactly those spots with fresh pale boards nailed in, tightens the seams and sands the old boards to honey. Tier 2 is polished oak; tier 3 walnut chevron parquet in a border. Washrooms: dingy tiles with a gap and a crack, then regrouted with bright new tiles, then checker, then marble. The lobby adds a doormat (tier 1), a waiting rug under the queue (tier 2) and a grand gold-bordered rug (tier 3). Damage never sits on a pad, a doorway, a guest spot or under furniture. Code: `src/world/Floors.ts`.
- **Rags to riches (session 4, owner request; simplified in session 7):** every carriage arrives run-down but clean and readable: weathered grey floorboards, dull plain walls, iron cots, bare bulbs, bare furniture (session 6's debris layer of gaps, puddles, peeling paint, grime and cobwebs was removed: the owner found it confusing). It is fixed first, then made pretty (Repaired: honey floorboards and clean walls → Cosy: carpets, colour → Luxurious: panelling, gold). A refurbish tile rebuilds the carriage in place with a makeover moment; each tier raises that carriage's earnings (sleeper fares, washroom tips, or train-wide tips for service cars). The train's **livery** follows the route level (Workshop Grey → Meadow Green → Midnight Navy → Royal Blue & Gold), so the train looks as famous as it is; since session 12 that paints the locomotive, the service cars and the rear deck, while each passenger carriage wears its class's livery (§6b), so the train reads as a mix of liveries.
- **Comforts (session 6):** between the big refits, each cabin carriage buys Reading Lamps, Fresh Flowers and Wireless Radios, and the washroom car Scented Soaps and Warm Towel Rails. Each pops into every room of that carriage and lifts its tips (+20% cabin tips, +25% washroom tips each). They share the carriage's one improvement spot with the refits, so only one improvement per carriage is on show at a time.
- **Refits in view (session 11, owner: "the cozy upgrade should come earlier… it's hidden behind a few blocks so you can't see its price"):** a carriage's next refit is never queued behind its other tiles: it takes the carriage's improvement spot as soon as it is available (one refit at a time, outside the tile cap, ahead of that carriage's comforts), so its price is always in view. The lobby's Cosy Makeover needs only the first coupling (180; lands ~7 min on the autopilot, was ~15), the sleeper's needs the second (260; ~15 min, was ~22). Code: `Tiles.refresh`.
- **Carriages work together (session 6, owner request):** the washroom car brings its own linen closet (towels and rolls), so it works the moment it couples; the stores car is automation (a runner and a stock of crates), never a requirement.
- **Precise, tactile control (session 9, owner request):** a stick response curve (slow, careful steps near the centre, full speed before the rim), firmer braking than acceleration, a doorway funnel (walk into the wall beside an opening and you slide into it) (`player` in `economy.ts`). Session 10: no footstep or creak sounds (the owner found them tacky).
- **Getting around (session 6, owner request):** holding a direction breaks into a stride (×1.55 after 0.45 s); tap-to-dash on the train map speeds up with distance (up to ×4); the camera frames the room you are in (a gentle zoom), pulls back on the platform and leads a little in the direction of travel. Staff wait between jobs in their own idle spot out of the walkways, and step aside for the conductor.
- **A wider train (session 14, owner: "a bit wider so there's more breathing area… not congested"):** carriages are 5.4 m across (`HALF_WIDTH` 2.7, was 2.2): a 1.44 m corridor, rooms 0.75 m wider, aisles in the service cars widened to match. The reception desk spans the queue's two rows, so the waiting rug lines up with it end to end, and a clear lane runs round the queue on every side. The track bed, verges, shoreline, boat lane, footpath and billboards moved out by half a metre (`TRACK_HALF` 3.0).
- **Tiles say what they are (session 14, owner: "the upgrade boxes are hidden and I have no idea what they are"):** every unlock tile has a floating marker over it: the icon, a short name ("Cabin 6", "Comfort", "Porter") and the price on a little pill with a pointer to the plate, drawn over walls at a constant size on screen, so a tile in a narrow room is never hidden. It steps aside while you stand on the tile and where the close-up label (name and effect) shows (`TileMarker` in `sprites.ts`).
- Room doorways are wide (1.4 m, session 9) and centred on each room's wall, so you walk straight in onto the room's work spot; bi-parting sliding doors open for whoever walks up and vanish into the wall; room sizes are proportionate (a train loo is small, a cabin fits a bed and a walk-in).
- **Everything in view, rooms easy to enter (session 15, owner: "the upgrade markers are still hidden… entering rooms is still a problem… remove the cupboard from the corridor… more room to walk"):** the camera looks over the train from one side and the rear, so walls facing it hid the floor behind them (refit tiles at every carriage end were 78% hidden). Walls between rooms are knee-high (0.52 m, `INTERIOR_WALL_HEIGHT`), each carriage's rear end wall is a low sill (`CUTAWAY_WALL_HEIGHT`), door leaves and gangway bellows follow; outer side walls keep their windows. `tests/visibility.test.ts` casts the camera's sight line over every wall and requires every tile fully visible and every pad at least 90% visible in every floor plan of every class (rear tiles at `REAR_TILE_X/Z` in a 1.5 m rear vestibule, tiles 1 m square, room pads nearer their front wall). The doorway funnel had a bug: a push straight at the wall beside a doorway "slid" by a floating-point crumb and never reached the funnel (pinned against washroom walls); head-on pushes now try it first, and `tests/entry.test.ts` walks into every room from anywhere in the corridor with the player's own movement rules (all in under a second). The lobby's corner piece (crates → cupboard → … → piano) moved from the corridor entrance to the front wall; the corridor is 1.62 m (was 1.44), everyone's collision radius 0.26 m (was 0.3).
- Walking must never feel like a tax: design carriages with short internal routes, give each carriage its own staff slot, and put supplies near where they are used. Speed upgrades should feel like a pleasure, not a necessity.

## 6b. Carriage luxury classes (session 12, owner request; protect this)
Every passenger carriage (the lobby car and the sleepers) climbs from **Basic** to **Royal Suite**, one cash refit at a time, and its class changes who travels in it, what they pay, what they ask for, how it looks inside and the colour it is painted outside. Service cars (washrooms, stores, luggage) keep their four refits (worn → Repaired → Cosy → Luxurious) and the route livery.

| Class | Refit tier | Offered at | Rooms (sleeper / lobby) | Fares / tips / stars | Adds | Livery | Inside |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Basic | 0 (worn), 1 (repaired, fares ×1.25) | start | 6 berths / 3 | ×1 / ×1 / 1 | cleaning | slate | narrow berths: broken boards, iron cots |
| Comfort | 2 | level 2 | 4 cabins / 2 | ×2.5 / ×2 / 2 | towels | teal | honey boards, a wider bed, a luggage bench, a fabric shade |
| Business | 3 | level 4 | 3 cabins / 2 | ×5 / ×3.5 / 3 | coffee | navy | parquet, a double bed, a wingback armchair, a writing desk, a minibar |
| First Class | 4 | level 6 | 2 suites / 2 | ×14 / ×7 / 5 | champagne, turndown | royal blue + gold | marble, a velvet bed, a lounge (sofa, table, a light over it) on a rug, champagne on ice, a piano in the lobby |
| Royal Suite | 5 | level 8 (the route's end) | 1 grand suite / 1 | ×40 / ×18 / 10 | the butler | burgundy + gold | the whole carriage: a four-poster, a slipper bath, a writing desk, an armoire, a dinner table for two and a grand piano, chandeliers |

- **Every class is its own floor plan (session 13, owner: "each class having different config, sizes and interior"):** fewer, bigger, grander rooms as the class rises (`LOBBY_ROOMS`/`SLEEPER_ROOMS` in `layout.ts`, `getLayout(type, tier)`; tiers 0–1 share the Basic plan). Fares rise faster than the rooms fall, so a full sleeper earns 7.5, 10, 15, 28 and 40 fares a ride from Basic to Royal: every refit is a clear step up. A new carriage comes as six bare berths you open one tile at a time (the lobby's third berth and the sleeper's fifth and sixth are new); the Comfort refit rebuilds the carriage into four cabins, all open, and settles any berth tiles left (anything paid into one comes back). A refit that changes the plan moves uncollected tips into the new rooms, has staff re-plan, and shows every guest to a room (theirs, another free one of their class, any free one, or a thank-you tip and on their way: nothing lost). Suites have a nav waypoint beside the bed, and what each class keeps at the foot of the bed is solid.

- **Guests have a class.** Travellers turn up for the classes the train sells (by its open cabins), plus a few aspirants for the next class up (`classes.aspirantChance`, 18%). The platform queue shows **class-coloured tickets**; a guest boards only a free bed in their own class, so aspirants are visible demand for the next refit (never a penalty, never counted against a perfect stop). Archetypes belong to a class (student and backpacker Basic; tourist, grandma, family Comfort; businessman Business; newlyweds, VIP, celebrity First; royalty Royal).
- **Each class adds one service:** Comfort guests ask for towels, Business for coffee, First for champagne and a **turndown** when they arrive (a short dwell at the bed), Royal for the **butler** (requests come two at a time and pay as one generous tip). Coffee and champagne come from the tea urn, towels from the linen closet. Staff learn each new service.
- **Class stars:** each request and each tidy room is worth 2 stars in Comfort, 3 in Business, 5 in First and 10 in the Royal Suite (fewer guests, more famous ones), so a classier train still climbs the league faster.
- **The upgrade moment:** a class refit runs the makeover wipe, which repaints the outside as it rebuilds the inside, then shows the class name and emblem and what it is worth ("Fares ×14") with a fanfare and sparkles along the new paint. A **class chip** (a navy pill with the class emblem on a disc of its colour, gold-rimmed for First and Royal) floats over the lake side of every passenger carriage and pops when it changes. New carriages couple as Basic.
- **Meta:** goals for each class (Comfort, Business, First Class, Royal Suite), front pages for First Class and the Royal Suite (Business is the walnut-and-brass page), coach cues for the first class tile and the first turndown, the level card lists the class each level brings, and the Gazette photo paints each carriage in its own class.
- Up to two refit tiles are on show at once, each on its own carriage (`tiles.maxRefits`), so a dear class refit on one carriage never hides the next class on another.
- Data: `src/config/classes.ts` (classes, liveries, multipliers, request pools), `classes` in `economy.ts`, class refits in `CARRIAGE_CATALOGUE` (`content.ts`), archetypes' `cls`. Code: `layout.ts` (`rooms()` furnishes by `RoomStyle`: berth, cabin, business, first, royal), `TrainState.classOf`/`classCapacity`/`relayoutCabins`/`retireBerthTiles`, `Guests` (boarding by class, turndown, butler, `rehome`), `ClassChips`, `CarriageView` (`buildClassDressing` with `buildPendant`, `buildGrandBed`, `buildBureau`, the armchair, armoire, dining table and grand piano in `buildProp`), suite rugs in `Floors.ts`.

## 7. Staff (automation)
Hireable per carriage via price tiles: **Porter** (luggage, boarding), **Attendant** (cleans cabins, restocks bathrooms), **Steward/Waiter** (dining car), **Supply runner** (carries supplies between cars), **Bartender**, **Stoker** (Alpine route). Staff have upgradable speed and carry capacity. Staff use simple, readable state machines with visible intent (they walk to a task, do it, return).

## 9. Progression — three nested clocks
1. **Seconds:** the next unlock tile (always 20–60 s away).
2. **Session:** the **route level** star bar. Every unlock, upgrade, request and clean station stop adds stars; a full bar levels the route and pays Rail Miles + cash (x2 with a rewarded ad). Route 1 maxes at level 8.
3. **Days:** new routes (roughly one every 1–2 hours of play), passenger stories, the postcard album, the season pass.

Introduce each layer only after the one below it is understood:
- **Session 5 (owner: "tight, addictive progression"):** level 2 comes at ~6:30; stars fly from where they were earned into the level ring, which fills as they land; near a level-up one toast says what the next level brings. **Rush:** services done by hand back to back (within 8 s) build a streak shown under the conductor, and milestones pay a cash bonus; a lapse costs nothing. Unlocks land with a camera kick and a beat of slow motion; a refurbishment sweeps a band of light down the carriage, revealing the new finish behind it.
- **Tutorial at the player's pace (session 9, owner: "the prompts go way too fast… actually doing the things should move to the next step"):** every walkthrough step and mechanic lesson stays until the player has done it (serving a request, tidying a room, boarding, restocking, buying the tile…), then a "Nice!" and a short rest before the next line. A lesson whose moment passes hides and returns next time; a chore the staff have taken over retires its lesson. Requests are taught in two parts (pick it up here, now bring it to the guest). Goals credit what the player did while the previous goal was up. The first build opens the lobby's second cabin (the starting carriage comes with one ready): intentional.
- **One layer at a time (session 15, owner: "the tutorials and challenges in the first 30 s–1 min are way too much… step by step"):** the four-step walkthrough has the screen to itself. The goal chain sleeps until the walkthrough is done, the first stop is behind and the train is named (then the goals the walkthrough already covered are settled, their rewards in one burst); lessons after the walkthrough are at least 9 s apart; Rush streaks start after the first coupling; class badges appear once there is a second carriage (or a class refit); passengers' reaction bubbles after the first stop; no offer chip before 2.5 minutes, and a soft offer stays 10 s with a 12 s rest before the next. The first coupling costs 120 (Rush no longer funds it): first carriage ~3:40, level 2 ~6:10 on the autopilot. Tunables: `onboarding` in `economy.ts`, `COACH_LESSON_GAP_SECONDS`.
- **Show, don't tell (session 11, owner: "too much text… the gameplay should be centre stage"):** the guide arrow and the glowing spot are the instruction. A coach cue is an icon plus at most three words ("Tidy up", "New carriage"), and the words fold away after 4 s (7 s in the first-minute walkthrough), leaving a bobbing icon until the player does it. A step done gets a tick, not a "Nice!". Goals are a compact chip (icon, bar, count, reward); their few words show for 3.5 s when new or when tapped. Pop-ups are numbers and icons (a tick for spotless, a bolt for speedy, a crossed-out towel for none left), toasts are two words or a number, the station banner is the name alone, the ticket is icon chips, cards (chooser, Rival Watch, front page) keep a title, a picture and the numbers. The UI audit enforces a text budget in live play: at most 3 words of two or more letters on screen on average, never more than 8 (cards excluded).
- **Objective chain (session 6, owner: "methodical… walk players through every mechanic"):** one MPH-style goal at a time in a banner under the top bar (46 goals from the first check-in to the Royal Suite), each paying a small reward that flies into the counter. Goals about what you own (tiles of a kind, refits, carriages, station upgrades, conductor levels) count everything bought so far, so none can stall; the Rail Miles goal opens its sheet when tapped. Data: `src/config/objectives.ts`.
- **Pacing (session 6, owner: "I finished it in 20 minutes"):** route 1 now takes about an hour to build out on the autopilot: couplings at ~3:40, ~11, ~21 and ~45 minutes, level 2 at ~7:40, a purchase every 20–130 s, level 8 after the last refit. Beds grow steadily (income follows beds) and prices follow income.
- **Pacing with classes (session 13, class floor plans):** on the autopilot, couplings at ~3:50, ~8:40, ~20:30 and ~29:40, level 2 at ~6:20, the lobby's Comfort refit at ~8:30 and the sleeper's at ~19 (after its six berths), Business from ~33 minutes, level 8 at ~51, First Class at ~52–53 and the Royal Suites at ~57 and ~61, the route's capstone (about an hour in all). Mastery tiles (a second staff upgrade at levels 6–7) fill the late game. The longest waits are now saving up for the two Royal Suites (~3.5 minutes each, with the tile filling as you pay), not a level gate.
- Daily requests/quests appear at route level 3.
- Daily login calendar and the Grand Tour track at route level 4.
- Passenger stories at route level 5.

## 11. Characters and meta (our edge over MPH)
- **Guests have personality**: a few archetypes with distinct silhouettes, colours and idle animations (businessman, backpacker, grandma, family, honeymooners, VIP in a fur coat).

## 12. Monetization (humane hybrid — better than MPH, still strong)
**Rewarded ads** (opt-in, contextual, shown at the moment of shortage; each can be paid with gems instead):
- Cash stash / VIP passenger who scatters cash.
- **Hold the train** (+15 s at a station).
- Speed boost: rail scooter / roller skates, +50% speed for 3 minutes.
- Double fares at the next station.
- Temporary porter for one stop.
- x2 route level-up reward; x2 daily login reward.
- Missing-supply delivery.

**Interstitials (forced) — strict rules:**
- Only during the **Departing** phase.
- Never before minute 10 of lifetime play.
- Never more often than one per 3 minutes (configurable), never two stations in a row early on.
- Never mid-task, never during a station stop, never after a rewarded ad in the same stop.
- All thresholds live in remote-configurable settings.

**Banners:** none in the first session; if used later, only on menu screens.

**Purchases:**
- **First Class Ticket**: removes forced ads **account-wide** + gems + Rail Miles.
- **Conductor's Scooter**: permanent speed vehicle + skin.
- **Gem packs** (6 tiers).
- **Grand Tour Pass**: account-wide seasonal pass with free and premium tracks (fixes MPH's per-hotel pass).
- **Carriage skin sets** (vintage, royal, neon) and conductor outfits. First step in the prototype: the **Paint Shop** (in the Shop) sells premium liveries for gems next to the ones earned by reputation; cosmetic only.

**Offer timing:** the First Class Ticket is first offered after the first satisfying milestone (not in the first 5 minutes), again at session starts, with a discount if ignored twice. Never interrupt a task with an offer.

<art_audio_feel>
- **Art:** stylised low-poly, flat-shaded with soft gradients, warm palette per route (Countryside: golden greens and cream; Alpine: whites and deep blues; Desert: sand, saffron and pink). Strong silhouettes readable at thumb size. Consistent, modular kit (floors, walls, beds, sinks, tables, doors) so new carriages are re-arrangements. Prototype uses gray-box primitives with a clean placeholder palette — gameplay first, art later, but the gray-box must still read clearly.
- **Lighting:** night is the default and hero look (session 12, owner request; `time.mode` in `config/visuals.ts` can bring back the day → dusk → night cycle): a soft lavender moonlit night (session 13: lifted so it reads, not murky), warm lamplight inside, the locomotive's headlight, lit smoke, fog in the distance, a gentle grade and a CSS vignette. **The lamplight is baked (session 13):** every room's warm fill, each lamp's pool (staying in its own room, a little through doorways), the light out of every window onto the water and the platform, and soft contact shading where walls, furniture and the hull meet the floor are painted into a top-down light map whenever the train changes (`lightBake.ts`, pure and unit-tested; `trainLight.ts`, `LightMap.ts`; tunables in `night.light`), so every tier gets the same lighting for one texture read.
- **Windows (session 13, owner: "so intrusive and not adjusted properly"):** framed windows (a sill, a header, posts and a sash bar standing proud of the paint) with warm lamplit glass set back in the outside face (curtains painted in from Comfort up), night glass on the room side, and no glowing top faces, so from above nothing smears; the light shows on the glass and spills out onto the ground through the light map. Window glass stays under the bloom threshold; so do specular highlights (a gold rail catching the moon gleams, never flares): only lamps bloom.
- **Smooth on phones (session 14, owner: "massive frame drops… it has to run smoothly on iPhone and Android"):** no frame does a big job. Icons are drawn once and copied (PNG-encoding them on first use stalled whole seconds); the scenery builds the next stretch a slice per frame; the light bake runs in slices with the old map showing; the platform and the outside dressing rebuild over several frames after a coupling (`core/Background.ts`, 2 ms a frame); the UI reads layout once per frame before writing and never forces a reflow. Shadows redraw every other frame on Low and Medium; phones start at Medium (iPhones had been read as weak by Safari's capped core count, or given High).
- **Top-notch on phones (session 15, owner: "slow and sluggish… takes time to pick up smoother framerates… performance top notch on Android and iPhone, and we'll add many more levels"):** phones render straight to the screen: Low and Medium have no post-processing (no half-float target, no bloom chain, no extra full-screen passes; the screen's own multisampling antialiases, nearly free on phone GPUs), bloom starts at High (session 14 had moved phones onto the post-processed Medium: the regression). Every character part (bodies, limbs, props, blankets, carried items, blob shadows) is an instance in a shared `BatchedMesh` (`world/CharacterBatch.ts`): one draw for the whole crowd however many people (a mid-game scene went from 212 draw calls to 104; the shadow pass halved). `Stage.warmUp` compiles every shader before the first frame (the refit wipe used to compile 5–6 mid-game; its clipped materials are now persistent clones), shader error checks only with dev tools. Frames are held to ~60 fps on 120/144 Hz screens (the Android app also asks the display for 60 Hz). Dynamic resolution judges the share of slow frames in a 0.6 s window: it steps down at once, climbs slowly, never back above a scale that failed, and remembers its scale per tier for the next launch (phones open at 0.88), so the start is smooth instead of settling for ~15 s. Spread-out work (scenery stretch, rebuilds, light bake) shares one 2.5 ms per-frame budget; static scenery matrices are frozen; HUD targets are measured four times a second.
- **One look on every quality tier (session 13, owner: "graphics quality is inconsistent"):** every tier uses the same physically based materials, the same light map, the same analytic sky reflection and moon glint (no environment map) and the same ACES tone map and grade (installed as the renderer's tone mapping, so Low matches the post-processed tiers). Low: no post-processing; Medium (the 3 GB target): bloom at a reduced size and FXAA; High: soft shadows, 4× MSAA, real lake reflections; Ultra: sharper reflections and screen-space ambient occlusion. Each tier caps its pixel count; while playing, the render scale drops (to 62%) before Auto ever drops a tier. Materials are recipes (`surfaces.ts`: matte, varnish, velvet, brass, glass…), baked into the vertex data so a carriage stays one draw per material.
- **Lakeside (session 12, rebuilt in session 13; owner: "the lake just stops out of nowhere and the mountains clip into the train"):** the shoreline, the ground's height and its colour are continuous functions of where you are (`terrain.ts`), repeating exactly every 2.4 km, so pieces join without a seam and the lake never ends; the water shader runs the same shoreline, so it knows its depth (a darker deep, the bed showing through the shallows, foam at the bank, the moon's sheen and glitter, the windows' light on the water, haze). The lake reaches to the far plane; the painted mountain card is gone (it cut through the train). Every 24 m chunk pairs a shore piece (reeds, beach, jetty, boathouse, lilies, promenade, lighthouse, fishing hut, island) with a land piece (forest, meadow, village, farm, orchard, lane, windmill, chapel, camp) dealt from shuffled decks with no repeat within four, filled with instanced trees, shrubs, rocks and flowers, lamp-post pools, turning sails and a sweeping lighthouse beam. Rules, unit-tested: nothing inside the track bed, nothing on the land side taller than the camera can see over the train, the boat lane kept clear for lantern boats and a pair of swans.
- **Juice (mandatory, not polish):** every action has a sound, a particle and a small screen reaction. Cash flies in arcs and stacks with a squash-and-stretch; unlock tiles fill with a satisfying radial and "pop" into existence with a scale bounce; coupling has camera shake and dust; haptics on key moments (toggleable).
- **Audio (session 10, owner: "the sound effects before were FAR better… the music is atrocious"):** effects are the warm synthesised set players liked (session 8 recipes, unchanged), each allowed to ring to its end (a cue already ringing its limit is skipped, never cut; a transparent peak limiter; fades on pause). No footsteps or creaks. The rail clack is the soft session 8 "da-dum". Music is the **Night Express theme** (session 10b, owner: "too spooky… we want chill vibes"): an original sunny lo-fi groove in F (80 bpm, swung sixteenths, 16 bars, 48 s loop): major-ninth piano chords stepping down B♭maj9–Am11–Gm9–Fmaj9, a round electric bass, a soft acoustic kit and a clean electric-guitar hook on the major pentatonic; nothing tense or dark. ## Engine and platform
- **Web build (current, since session 2):** TypeScript (strict) + **Three.js**, bundled by esbuild into **one self-contained HTML file** that plays in the Claude app (as an Artifact) and in any phone browser. Portrait only. No runtime dependencies beyond Three.js; effects are synthesised with WebAudio, the music is one small MP3 inlined in the file, icons are drawn on canvas.
- **App Store and Google Play (session 15):** Capacitor 8 wraps the same page (`dist/app/index.html`) in native iOS and Android apps (`ios/`, `android/`, `capacitor.config.ts`); the game reaches the native side only through `src/services/native.ts` (save mirroring, haptics, lifecycle, back button, status bar, launch screen). Building, signing and submitting: `NATIVE.md`.
- The original plan was **Unity 6 LTS / URP / C#** (Android first, then iOS); that M0 skeleton is kept in commit `864b131`. The architecture below (pure sim layer, service interfaces, data-driven config) ports cleanly if a publisher wants native.
- Target device: a 3 GB RAM mid-range Android. **60 fps** with a full 10-carriage train and ~30 characters. Cold start under 5 s. Size: session 13, the owner allows a much bigger build (around 300 MB) rather than giving up quality; the single file is 1.6 MB today, so there is room for real textures, recorded audio or a glTF kit when they pay off.
- Fully playable **offline**.

## Architecture principles
1. **Data-driven.** Every number that designers tune (prices, timers, speeds, capacities, rewards, ad rules) lives in ScriptableObjects or a single economy table — never hard-coded. Changing balance must not require code changes.
2. **Modular and decoupled.** Systems talk through a lightweight event bus or C# events and interfaces. No god objects, no scene-wide `FindObjectOfType` in gameplay loops.
3. **Service interfaces for everything external.** Ads, IAP, analytics and remote config sit behind interfaces (`IAdService`, `IIAPService`, `IAnalyticsService`, `IRemoteConfig`) with **mock implementations** now; the publisher's SDKs plug in later without touching gameplay code.
4. **Performance by default.** Object pooling for guests, cash, particles and items; no per-frame allocations in hot paths; cached component references; GPU instancing for repeated props; baked or minimal lighting; simple colliders.
5. **Deterministic and testable** where cheap: pure C# classes for economy math and state machines, so they can be unit tested with the Unity Test Framework.
6. **Save system** from day one: JSON with a **version number and migrations**, autosave on key events and on app pause/quit, and **offline earnings** on return (capped, with a x2 rewarded option).
